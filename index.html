Hip-Hop dance

Birth of hip-hop: It started in the Bronx, New York City, born from block parties. It was an expression of life and struggle in a culture marked by hardship, and also of resilience and creativity.

Hip-hop dance is a style of street dance that grew from hip-hop culture and music. It borrows from African dance, tap, and ballet. It began as freestyle in the streets by dance crews, and today may include choreography. Classic moves include the running man and the humpty dance.

Cheerdance

Cheer + Dance: to cheer is to shout words or phrases that motivate and boost the morale of a playing team. Dance is a physical activity expressing emotions or gestures through body movement, usually in time with rhythm.

Origin: cheerdance came from cheerleading, a routine of jumps, tumbling, lifts, tosses, and shouted cheers that lead the crowd. It originated in the United States.

Chants vs. cheers

Chant

Very short. Repeated 3 or more times.

Cheer

Longer than a chant. Done only 1 time.
Offense Scoring; used when your team has the ball. “We’ve got the ball, let’s score!”
Defense Taking the ball away; used when your team does not have it. “T-A-K-E take that ball away.”
Spirit / General Any time. “Let’s go Bears!”

    ⚡ 60-second recap

    Hip-hop started in the Bronx, New York City. Its five key elements are rapping, beatboxing, turntablism, breakdancing, and graffiti. The styles are popping, shuffling, locking, B-boying, tutting, krumping, waacking, and freestyle.

    Cheerdance comes from cheerleading, which began in the United States. For arm positions, remember up, out, and down. High V, Touchdown, and Overhead Clasp are up. T, Diagonal, Half T, and L are out. Low V, Low Touchdown, and Low Clasp are down. A chant is short and repeated three or more times. A cheer is longer and done once. Safety first: bases lock their arms, the spotter holds the flyer, and everyone lands with feet together.
